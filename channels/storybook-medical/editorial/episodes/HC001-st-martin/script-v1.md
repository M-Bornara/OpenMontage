## S1 — He Survived a Hole in His Stomach
`[excited]` In 1822, a musket went off less than a meter from Alexis St. Martin. [C02]
He was about eighteen. A canoe-man for the fur trade, on Mackinac Island. [C01]
The shot tore open his left side.
Half an hour later, the army surgeon arrived… and could see straight into his stomach. [C03]
`[curious]` For seventeen days, everything Alexis swallowed came back out… through the wound. [C04]
He should have died.
He didn't.
But the hole wouldn't close. The doctor tried for months. [C05]
The only option left was to stitch it shut.
Alexis said NO. [C05]
`[whispers]` So his stomach did something strange. A fold of its lining grew over the hole… like a little lid. [C06]
He lived with that opening for fifty-eight more years. [C07]
`[curious]` And his doctor had just realised what it could teach him.

## S2 — The Doctor Who Put Food on a String
`[excited]` A doctor once lowered dinner into a living man's stomach. On a string. [C08]
August first, 1825. Army surgeon William Beaumont. His patient had survived a gunshot… that left a permanent opening into his stomach. [C01–C06]
Nobody really knew how digestion worked. Did the stomach grind food? Did food just… rot? [C12]
So Beaumont tied six foods to a silk string. Spiced beef. Salt pork. Raw beef. Boiled beef. Stale bread. Raw cabbage. [C08]
At noon… in they went.
`[curious]` One hour later, he pulled the string. The bread and cabbage? Half gone. [C09]
Two hours: the cabbage, bread, pork and boiled beef had vanished from the string. [C09]
Then his patient felt sick. So Beaumont dropped the medicine… straight in through the hole. [C10]
`[mischievously]` Medical ethics committees were not exactly a thing yet.
And that was experiment number ONE… of two hundred thirty-eight. [C11]

*~120 words. Visual comedy: a pocket watch at 12:00 → 1:00 → 2:00 while the string comes out with fewer
items each time (illustrated, clean). Pills dropping in shown as a simple diagram.*

## S3 — The Human Experiment Who Walked Away
`[curious]` The most famous stomach in medical history belonged to a man who kept walking away.
Alexis St. Martin couldn't read or write. A gunshot had left an opening into his stomach, and his doctor, William Beaumont, wanted to study it. [C21]
In 1825, Alexis left. Back home to Canada. "Without my consent," Beaumont wrote. [C16]
Four years later, fur-company agents brought him back. With a wife and two kids. Two thousand miles. [C17]
Between experiments he chopped wood and carried loads, as the doctor's servant. [C18]
In 1833 he signed a contract: a "covenant servant", who must submit to experiments "on or in" his stomach. One hundred fifty dollars a year. [C20]
`[whispers]` Then his wife said no. [C22]
For eighteen years, Beaumont sent letters. Bigger offers. Even his own son. [C23]
"Don't disappoint me more," he wrote. [C24]
Alexis never went back.

close-up (illustrated, period hand); the letters stack up with dates; last shot: Alexis on his own farm,
turning away from a rider.*
