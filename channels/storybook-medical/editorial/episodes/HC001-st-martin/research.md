## Timeline (what the record says)
| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 6 Jun 1822 | St. Martin, a French-Canadian **voyageur** (canoe-man) for the American Fur Company, about 18, is "accidentally wounded by the discharge of a **musket**". The charge was "powder and **duck shot**", from "not more than **one yard**" away, into his left side. | P1 p.9 |
| same day | Beaumont sees him 25–30 min later. Part of the lung and the stomach are pushing out of the wound, and the stomach is "pouring out the food he had taken for his breakfast". | P1 p.10 |
| first 17 days | Everything he swallows comes back out through the wound, so he is fed by enemas. | P1 p.11 |
| 1822–23 | Beaumont tries "for eight or ten months" to close the opening. Stitching it shut was the only option left, "**an operation to which the patient would not submit**". | P1 p.14 |
| winter 1823–24 | A fold of stomach lining forms a natural **valve** over the hole. Pressing it with a finger opens it. | P1 p.17 |
| May 1825 | First experiments at Fort Mackinac. They continue at Fort Niagara in June. | P1 p.17 |
| 1 Aug 1825 | **Experiment 1.** Beef, pork, bread and raw cabbage on a **silk string** go in at noon. At 1 pm the cabbage and bread are half digested. At 2 pm the cabbage, bread, pork and boiled beef are "cleanly digested, and gone from the string". St. Martin feels sick, so Beaumont drops calomel pills **straight into the stomach through the hole**. | P1 pp.125–127 |
| Aug 1825 | At Plattsburgh, St. Martin "returned to Canada, his native place, **without obtaining my consent**". | P1 p.18 |
| 1825–29 | In Canada for 4 years: he marries, has two children, and works as a voyageur for the Hudson's Bay Company in 1827–28. | P1 p.18 |
| Aug 1829 | Fur-company agents bring him, his wife and two children "nearly two thousand miles" to Fort Crawford, Prairie du Chien, "at great expense to me". | P1 pp.18–19 |
| 1829–Mar 1831 | Second series of experiments. Between experiments "he performed all the duties of a common servant, chopping wood, carrying burthens". | P1 p.19 |
| spring 1831 | The family goes home **in an open canoe**: down the Mississippi, up the Ohio, across Ohio, over the Great Lakes and down the St. Lawrence to Montreal. | P1 p.19 |
| 1832 | He lives through the cholera epidemic in Canada. | P1 pp.19–20 |
| Nov 1832–Nov 1833 | Washington experiments. He is enrolled as **Sergeant Alexis St. Martin**, an orderly at the War Department. | P1 p.20, P2 |
| 19 Oct 1833 | **Contract.** He is Beaumont's "covenant servant" for one year, and must "submit to… such philosophical or medical experiments as the said William shall direct… on or in the stomach of him". Pay: board, lodging and **$150 a year**. A second contract on 7 Nov 1833 runs 2 years for $400. | P2 |
| 1833 | **238 numbered experiments** across four series (4 + 56 + 116 + 62), published as the book. | P1, verified from the numbering |
| Jun 1834 | A letter, signed with his mark: "his wife was not willing to let him go". He never works for Beaumont again. | P2 |
| 1836–1852 | Letters, offers and messengers. In 1846 Beaumont's son Israel goes to fetch him, but Alexis has his own farm near Berthier and won't come. In 1851–52 Beaumont offers $500 a year. | P2 |
| 15 Oct 1852 | Beaumont's last letter, "Mon Ami": "Don't disappoint me more nor forfeit the bounties and blessings reserved for you." | P2 |
| 1840 | The Medical Society of London reportedly raises £300–400 to bring him to London. Paris also takes an interest. | P2 |
| 25 Apr 1853 | Beaumont dies in St. Louis after a fall. Accounts differ on the details (icy steps, head or hip). | Wikipedia + popular sources; Tier C |
| 1856 | St. Martin is studied in Philadelphia by Dr. F. G. Smith. At times he "traveled about exhibiting the wound" to doctors and students. His comrades called him "the man with a lid on his stomach". | P2 |
| 24 Jun 1880 | Dies at St-Thomas de Joliette, Quebec. Osler (then in Montreal) writes "offering to pay a fair sum for the stomach" for the Army Medical Museum, without success. | P2 |
| 28 Jun 1880 | Buried. Per the Curé's account relayed by Judge Baby, the family "kept the body at home much longer than usual and during a hot spell of weather, so as to allow decomposition to set in", and had "the grave dug **eight feet** below the surface… to prevent any attempt at a resurrection". | P2 (attributed) |

## Science (what they actually learned)
- Beaumont described gastric juice as "a clear, transparent fluid… a little saltish; and very perceptibly
  acid", and found it dissolves food **outside the body too**: meat in vials of juice, kept warm, digested
  slowly. This supported **chemical** digestion by an acid juice, not just grinding or rotting. (P1 pp.85ff;
  Level 1 physiology. Modern framing: the stomach both churns and chemically digests.)
- Beaumont wrote that he put his **tongue to the stomach lining**: "On applying the tongue to the mucous coat
  of the stomach, in its empty, unirritated state, no acid taste can be perceived. When food… [has] been
  applied… the acid taste is immediately perceptible." (P1 p.104) **"He tasted the inside of his stomach"
  is therefore ATTRIBUTED (Beaumont's own report), not a myth.**
- Mood and digestion: Beaumont recorded the subject's "anger and impatience" during experiments, and
  believed "violent passion" disturbed digestion. (P1 pp.153–155)

## Myth vs record
| Popular version | Record |
|---|---|
| "a shotgun blast" | Beaumont: a **musket** loaded with duck shot. Saying "a gun" is safe; "shotgun" is loose. |
| "fur trader" | He was a **voyageur** (paddler/labourer), not a trader. |
| "the wound never closed (by itself)" | Beaumont tried for months to close it. St. Martin **refused the stitching operation**. Some modern commentators (S2) suggest Beaumont was happy to keep it open; that is speculation. |
| "the doctor tasted his stomach" | True as Beaumont reported it (tongue on the lining). |
| "the family let the body rot and buried him 8 ft deep" | Attributed to the parish priest's account via Judge Baby (P2). Not proven, but a contemporary local account. Phrase it as "according to the parish priest". |
| "238 experiments" | Verified from the book's numbering. |
| "he ran away" | 1825: left "without obtaining my consent". 1831: released with a promise to return. 1834: his wife didn't want him to go. |
| ages | He was "about eighteen" in 1822 (P1), "28" in 1833 (army record) and "83" at death (Judge Baby). These are inconsistent, so **avoid stating his age at death.** |

## Human angles (people first)
- He couldn't read or write (letters "signed with his mark"). The contract was drafted by his employer and
  required him to submit to experiments.
- He refused surgery on his own body, and he kept choosing his family and home over the money.
- He paddled his family home across half a continent in an open canoe.
- Beaumont's obsession and persistence: his letters, his son sent to fetch him, rising offers.
- Ridicule ("the man with a lid on his stomach"), and the doctors who wanted his stomach even after he died.
