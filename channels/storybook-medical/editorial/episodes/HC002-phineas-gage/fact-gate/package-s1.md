Near Cavendish, Vermont, 13 September 1848. [C01] Phineas Gage, 25, was a railroad construction foreman, and his employers thought him the most efficient and capable foreman they had. [C01 C18] His job: pack blasting powder into holes in the rock with a smooth iron tamping bar, 3 ft 7 in long and 13¼ lb (1.1 m, 6 kg). [C23 C02]

His men caught his attention, he looked over his shoulder, and the iron struck a spark on the rock. [C03] The powder exploded. [C03] The bar went in under his left cheekbone, passed behind his left eye and came out through the top of his head. [C04] It landed about 80 feet (24 m) away. [C04]

Within minutes he spoke. [C05] He rode to his hotel sitting up in an ox cart, and Dr Williams later remembered his greeting: "Doctor, here is business enough for you." [C05 C06] He survived, and he kept the iron. [C32 C07] But his friends said he was "no longer Gage". [C08] What that really meant is a longer story. [NF] This is a history story, not medical advice.
