# HCS11 — Jean Hilliard, "Frozen Stiff, She Woke Up" · research

Researched 2026-11-18. Research only: no script yet. Claims log: `claims.csv` (C01–C44).

**Thumbnail sentence:** A 19-year-old lay in the snow for six hours at 22 below, so stiff that needles broke, and by noon she was talking.
**Family:** Impossible Survival. **Format:** Story Short.
**Core rating:** DOCUMENTED. A contemporary wire report (UPI, 31 Dec 1980) names her, the finder, the doctor,
the place and the outcome. AP covered it in January 1981. She and the finder retold it on the record (MPR 2018,
FOX 9 2022). NOAA station data fit the reported cold. Only the most dramatic details (the "frozen solid" label,
the body-temperature number, the pulse number) are weak or disputed.

**Overlap note:** HCS03 (Anna Bågenholm, 13.7 °C) is also a cold-survival story. HCS03's research already names
Hilliard as a possible "survival universe" follow-up. Keep the angle different: night walk + "frozen stiff" + a
rural hospital with only heating pads, not cardiac bypass.

## Human angles
- **Stakes:** a teenager alone on a gravel road at 22 below, in cowboy boots and thin gloves, at midnight (P5, P6).
- **The choice:** stay with the car, or walk to a friend she thinks is half a mile away. She walks. It is two miles (P6).
- **Escalation:** two farmhouses, nobody home (P1). Hill after hill: "thinking his place would be there, and it wasn't" (P5). "More frustrated than scared" (P5).
- **The fall:** she sees the house through the trees, falls, crawls, and stops 15 ft from the door (P1, P5, S1). Last memory: the driveway (P1).
- **The cost:** six hours. Nelson: "I thought she was dead… but I saw a few bubbles coming out of her nose" (P5). Doctors feared she would lose both legs (P5, P6).
- **The payoff:** heating pads, a prayer chain, and by noon she is talking (P5). Seven weeks later she walks out with both legs (P6). "It's like I fell asleep and woke up in the hospital" (P5). "I didn't see the light or anything like that. It was kind of disappointing" (P5): a good, light ending line.
- **Twist option:** "frozen solid" was the headline; a 1981 surgeon said it was cold muscle, not ice (P2).

  ditches, farmyards with a mailbox at the road (S1: mailbox 1/8 mile from the house). Night, snow cover, clear and

## Privacy note
Jean Hilliard is a private person who chose to tell her story in public (Today show 1981, *Unsolved Mysteries*
1996, MPR 2018, FOX 9 2022, Forum 2025). Use her 1980 name, her age, the event and her own quoted words. Do not use
her married name, current town, job, children or family details, even though the press printed some of them.
Wally Nelson spoke on the record (UPI 1980, MPR 2018): his name and quotes are fine. Don't name Edgar Sather or the
nurses.

## Advertiser safety
- Not a death story: she lives and recovers. Keep the "thought she was dead" beat brief.
- Never draw blackened or frostbitten limbs. "Black from the waist down" stays in narration at most, or is cut.
- Needles: a bent needle tip only, no puncture or blood.
- No alcohol (the dance/drinking detail is unsourced anyway).
- Prayer: report the prayer chain as an event; do not say it caused the recovery.
- Medical lines: attribute ("her doctor said", "doctors have a saying"). No advice about rewarming.

## Verdict
**PASSES the documented-core rule.** The central events (crash, two-mile walk at about −22 °F, collapse near the
door, ~6 hours outside, found rigid and barely alive, warmed with heating pads, talking by noon, kept her legs, full
recovery) are DOCUMENTED by a contemporary UPI report plus her and the finder's later accounts, and the cold is
backed by NOAA data. Do not use "frozen solid" as fact, any body-temperature number or any pulse number.
