# HCS09 — The Doctor They Hated for Saying "Wash Your Hands" · research + script v1 (approved 2026-11-06)

**Thumbnail sentence:** In 1847, a doctor told other doctors to wash their hands, and it cost him everything.
**Family:** History Got It Wrong. **Format:** Story Short. **Core rating:** DOCUMENTED (the clinic records,
his 1861 book). "Nobody listened" is an OVERSTATEMENT: some doctors did use the washing, and he waited 14 years
to publish. The script says "many doctors", and the nuance goes in the description.

## Sources
- **P1** — I. Semmelweis, *Die Ätiologie, der Begriff und die Prophylaxis des Kindbettfiebers* (1861), with the clinic tables.
- **S1** — Wikipedia, "Ignaz Semmelweis" (with references). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaz_Semmelweis

## Script v1 (~47 s)
```
[curious] In 1847, a doctor told other doctors to wash their hands. They hated him for it.
Vienna had two maternity wards. In the doctors' ward, about one mother in ten died of fever.
In the midwives' ward… far fewer. Women begged not to go to the doctors.
Then Ignaz Semmelweis's friend cut his finger during an autopsy… and died of the same fever.
The doctors were going straight from the dead… to the mothers.
So Semmelweis made them wash their hands in chlorine. Deaths fell to about one in a hundred.
[serious] But many doctors refused to believe their own hands could kill. He lost his job.
In 1865, he died in an asylum… of an infected wound.
[curious] Today, rejecting new evidence has a name… the Semmelweis reflex.
```
**Fact meter:** DOCUMENTED (the numbers) → OVERSTATEMENT ("nobody listened").
