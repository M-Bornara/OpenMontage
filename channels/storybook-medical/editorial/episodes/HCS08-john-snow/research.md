# HCS08 — The Doctor Who Stopped a Plague by Breaking a Pump · research + script v1 (for approval)

**Thumbnail sentence:** In 1854, a London doctor stopped a cholera outbreak by taking the handle off a water pump.
**Family:** Crazy Science (the detective). **Format:** Story Short. **Core rating:** DOCUMENTED (Snow's own book,
Whitehead's report, the parish minutes). The famous version ("the handle came off and the outbreak stopped") is
an OVERSTATEMENT, and Snow said so himself: that is the twist.

## Sources

## Facts
| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Soho, London, late Aug – Sept 1854: a sudden cholera outbreak around Broad Street | VERIFIED (S1) |
| "Upwards of five hundred fatal attacks" within about 250 yards, in ten days | VERIFIED (P1); totals for the whole outbreak vary (S1 gives 616 in some editions, other counts lower). The script says "about five hundred… in ten days" |
| Most doctors blamed "miasma", bad air | VERIFIED (S1) |
| Snow suspected the water; he traced the deaths and found them around the Broad Street pump | VERIFIED (P1, S1) |
| The Broad Street brewery's workers didn't catch it: they had a beer allowance and didn't drink the pump water | VERIFIED (S1, P1) |
| A widow in Hampstead, miles away, died after drinking Broad Street water she had delivered because she liked its taste; her niece died too | VERIFIED as told by Snow (P1); her name (Susannah Eley) and how the water reached her vary, so the script names no one |
| He showed the parish Board of Guardians on Thu 7 Sept; the handle was removed on 8 Sept | VERIFIED (S1) |
| Snow wrote the attacks "had so far diminished before the use of the water was stopped" | VERIFIED (P1 via S1) |
| The source: a baby's nappies washed into a cesspool next to the well (found by Whitehead) | VERIFIED (S1, S3). Not in the script (no room); a pinned-comment fact |

## Script v1 (~45 s)
```
[curious] In 1854, a doctor stopped a deadly outbreak… by taking the handle off a water pump.
London, Soho. Cholera killed about five hundred people in ten days.
Most doctors blamed bad air.
John Snow blamed the water. He went door to door, listing every death.
They clustered around one pump… on Broad Street.
The brewery next to it had no cases. The workers drank beer.
And a widow miles away died too. She liked the taste of Broad Street water… and had it brought to her.
On the eighth of September, they took the handle off.
[mischievously] The twist? The outbreak was already fading. Snow admitted it himself.
[curious] But the map he drew of those deaths… is how we still hunt diseases today.
```
**Fact meter:** DOCUMENTED (the pump) → OVERSTATEMENT ("the handle stopped it").
**Look:** 1854 Soho, crowded brick streets in fog, a black iron hand pump, Snow with a notebook at doorways,
brewery men with tankards, the handle lifted off by a workman, the dot map growing on paper. Cholera shown as
empty beds and closed shutters, never bodies. Matt's glasses: on the pump's trough.
**Pinned comment:** "The real source? A baby's nappy washed into a cesspool three feet from the well. Did you know? 👶"
