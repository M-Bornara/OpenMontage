# HCS10 — He Drank Cholera to Prove a Point · research

Researched 2026-11-18. Research only: no script yet. Claims log: `claims.csv` (44 rows).

**Thumbnail sentence:** A 73-year-old professor drank pure cholera germs to prove his rival wrong, and lived.
**Family:** Crazy Science. **Format:** Story Short. **Core rating:** DOCUMENTED (his own written account,
reported in the BMJ within six weeks, his 1893 paper, and a 1901 obituary all agree on the act, the date,
the soda, the dose and the mild illness).

## Human angles
- **Stakes:** Hamburg had just lost about 8,600 people (S8, S9). The man who had built German hygiene saw his
  life's theory collapse under him (S7, S3).
- **The choice:** Friends offered to take the risk instead of "their old teacher". He said no: test it on the
  "worthless body", his own. He was old, had sugar in his urine and "not a single tooth left" (P1).
- **The act:** baking soda first, then 1 cc of germs from the Hamburg outbreak. "It tasted like the purest
  water" (P1).
- **The cost:** two days later, cramps and diarrhoea for about a week. His pupil Emmerich tried it ten days
  later and got much sicker (P2).
- **The payoff:** both sides claimed victory. He said germs alone are harmless. His critics said he had just had
  a mild cholera. History sided with Koch on water (S17). But his clean-soil and sewer work did make Munich
  safer (P1), and some epidemiologists now credit his "germ plus environment" idea (S3).
- **Twist:** the baking soda he drank did not make the test fairer. It made it more dangerous (S15).

## Advertiser safety
- Never frame it as "drink this" or a dare. Say the germs came from a lab, and give no amounts of soda or culture
  on screen. Add a line, or keep the framing, that it was dangerous, and his student got much sicker.
- No stool images, no toilet, and no "rice-water". Show the lab result as a microscope slide. Cut the Isar
  sewage detail (P3).
- Don't say "suicide". The quote can stop at "in the service of science, like a soldier on the field of battle."
- Keep his death in 1901 out completely.
- Hamburg: say "thousands died" or "about 8,600". No corpses, use empty streets and water carts.

## Verdict
**PASS.** The central events are DOCUMENTED: the act, the date, the soda, the dose, the mild illness, Emmerich's
repeat and both sides' conclusions. They rest on Pettenkofer's own account (P1, P3) and a contemporary journal
report (P2), with scholarly confirmation (S2, S3, S4, S17). Only *why* he survived is DISPUTED, and the script
must present it as open, leading with "most experts think he caught a mild case". Do not fall back to the next
topic.

Before the script: (1) use "73", not 74; (2) don't say Koch sent the culture; (3) use the "field of battle"
wording, attributed; (4) if possible, a library check of the German MMW 1892 text for the original quote.
