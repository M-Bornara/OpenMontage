Munich, 7 October 1892. [C01] Max von Pettenkofer, 73, drank a fresh culture of cholera germs in front of witnesses. [C01 C02 C08 C09]

Cholera had just killed about 8,600 people in Hamburg. [C06] Robert Koch blamed a germ carried in the drinking water. [C05] Pettenkofer believed the germ alone was not enough: the soil and the ground water had to be right too. [C04] Some of his students offered to take the risk in place of their old teacher. [C18] He refused. [C18]

First he drank a little bicarbonate of soda, to neutralise his stomach acid. [C12] Then one cubic centimetre of a broth culture made in Munich from cultures of the Hamburg outbreak, prepared by Georg Gaffky, Koch's former assistant. [C14 C09 C10] He wrote that it tasted like the purest water. [C17]

Two days later came stomach cramps and diarrhoea, for about a week, and the lab found his gut swarming with cholera germs. [C19 C20] But it never turned dangerous, and he declared victory. [C19 C25] His critics said he had simply had a mild case of cholera, and most experts now think so too. [C26 C30] Ten days after him, his pupil Rudolf Emmerich repeated the drink and got much sicker, but survived. [C22] And the irony: modern studies show that neutralising stomach acid makes cholera far easier to catch. [C33] The soda made his experiment more dangerous, not less. [C33]

He lost the argument about the water. [C36] But his clean-water and sewer work did help Munich. [C36] This is a history story, not medical advice. [NF] Never try anything like this. [NF]
