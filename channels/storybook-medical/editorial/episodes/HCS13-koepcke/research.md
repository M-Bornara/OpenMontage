# HCS13 — She Fell From the Sky and Walked Out of the Jungle · research

Researched 2026-11-18. Free web sources only. Claims log: `claims.csv` (C01–C51).

**Thumbnail sentence:** A 17-year-old fell out of a breaking plane over the Amazon, woke under her seat row, and walked out of the jungle alone eleven days later.
**Family:** Impossible Survival. **Format:** Story Short. **Core rating:** DOCUMENTED (her own 1973 account,

## Human angles
- **Stakes:** 17, nearsighted, no glasses, one sandal, a broken collarbone, alone in the rainforest, and her mother somewhere in the wreck (P1, P3, S1).
- **The choice:** She didn't wait at the wreck. She found a trickle of water and followed it, because her parents had taught her that small streams lead to bigger rivers and to people (P1, P3, S1).
- **The cost:** Search planes passed overhead and couldn't see her (P1, P3). Insects laid eggs in her wounds (P3). She found a seat row of the dead and checked whether her mother was there (S1). She had nightmares "for years" (P3).
- **The payoff:** On about day ten a boat, a hut; next day three men walk out of the trees: "What have we here?" (P1). She became a biologist and now runs the jungle station her parents built (S1).
- **Her own line:** "The thought – why was I the only survivor? – haunts me. It always will." (P3)

## Care note
- She is alive (married name Juliane Diller). Use only what she has published or said on record. Quote her, don't invent her thoughts.
- Her mother, Maria Koepcke, was a respected ornithologist (birds are named after her). Say only "her mother did not survive." No speculation about her mother's last days, no body, no date of recovery.
- Don't depict the dead passengers. Don't show her injuries in close-up.
