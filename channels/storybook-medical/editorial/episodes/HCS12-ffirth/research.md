# HCS12 — The Student Who Drank Yellow Fever Vomit (Stubbins Ffirth) · research

Researched 2026-11-18. Research only: no script, no images, no paid calls.

**Thumbnail sentence:** To prove yellow fever wasn't catching, a Philadelphia medical student drank his patients' black vomit, and never got sick.
**Family:** Crazy Science. **Format:** Story Short. **Core rating:** DOCUMENTED. Every exposure is in his own
1804 thesis, numbered, with doses, and the first one dated (4 Oct 1802). A JAMA editorial (1964) confirms the routes
and that he never got sick. The reason he never got sick (the mosquito) is settled science.

## Human angles (sources only)
- **Stakes:** fear. He writes that the sick were abandoned: "the wife shuns the chamber of her husband, the husband of his
  wife, children of their parents, and parents of their children" (P1 p.46). He believed fear itself caused deaths and that
  proving the fever wasn't catching would cut deaths by half (p.46). Quarantine was also choking trade (p.11, p.47).
- **The choice:** he had first believed it was contagious (Preface). Then he slept in patients' beds and let them breathe
  in his face (p.50), and went from a dog to his own arm.
- **The cost:** a cut arm, over twenty more cuts, a burning eye, two hours in a 100 °F room with a pounding head, and then
  the cup. He notes that tasting and smelling it beforehand was what kept him from throwing up (p.56).
- **The payoff:** nothing happened. "How is it possible that I should never have the least effect produced?" (p.56).
  He wrote the thesis and declared it was never contagious, and "never can be" (p.59).
- **The twist:** he was right that touching didn't spread it, and wrong about why. The carrier was a mosquito: Finlay
  proposed it in 1881, sixty-one years after Ffirth died; Reed confirmed it in 1900. Reed's men even repeated the "sleep with
  the bedding" test for 20 nights, and also stayed well (S7).
- **After:** a doctor in South Carolina, he named his second son after his teacher Caspar Wistar and died at 38 on
  4 July 1820 (S5). The same gravestone calls him a "Master", which points to slaveholding: don't make him a pure hero.

## Advertiser safety
- **Gross factor: describe, never show.** No vomit on screen, no liquid in the eye, no wound close-ups, no act of
  drinking in close-up: show the cup, the face, the pulse.
- **Animals:** never show the dog or cat eating, and never the injected dog (it died). Best left out.
- **Racist line:** p.46 contains a slur about Black nurses. Never quote past "parents of their children".
- **No glorifying:** "Don't try this" is unnecessary, but never present it as a good idea; the twist (he was wrong) does that.
- Medical risk low: yellow fever is mosquito-borne and vaccine-preventable today (S6); no advice given.

## Verdict on the documented-core rule
**PASS.** The central events are DOCUMENTED in his own published thesis: every exposure, the doses, the first date, the
symptoms and the conclusion, confirmed in outline by JAMA 1964. "He never got sick" is his own report (no outside
witness, but it is the published record and no source disputes it). The explanation (mosquito) is settled science; the
"late-stage samples" part is LIKELY and must be said with "probably". No fallback needed (Carrión not researched).
