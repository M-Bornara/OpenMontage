# HC003 — Shorts, script v1 (for review)

## S1 — The Woman Who Couldn't Stop Dancing
`[curious]` In 1518, a woman stepped into a street in Strasbourg… and started to dance. [C01–C02]
No music. No party. She just… danced.
According to the chronicles, her name was Frau Troffea. [C02]
She kept going for hours, until she collapsed. The next morning, she got up… and danced again.
Her husband begged her to stop. She didn't. [C03]
`[excited]` Four days. Five. Six. On swollen feet, while a crowd gathered to watch. [C03]
And then… people in the crowd started dancing too.
Within days, more than thirty were dancing in the streets. [C04]
By the end of the summer, one chronicle counted FOUR hundred. [C05]
Frau Troffea was loaded into a wagon… and taken to a saint's shrine to be cured. [C06]
`[whispers]` But the city had already come up with a plan of its own.

*~120 words. Visuals: a cobbled lane, half-timbered houses, a lone figure dancing (graceful, uncanny, not
grotesque); the crowd grows in layers; a counter "30… 400*" with the asterisk "*one chronicle".*

## S2 — The City Hired Musicians. It Made Things Worse.
`[curious]` In 1518, Strasbourg tried to cure a dancing plague… by hiring a band. [C08]
Dozens of people were dancing in the streets and couldn't stop. So the city asked its doctors.
Their verdict: "a natural disease, from overheated blood." The cure? Let them dance it out. [C07]
`[excited]` So the council built stages in the horse market and the grain market. Opened guild halls. Paid drummers, pipers, horn players… and strong men to hold the dancers up. [C08]
`[mischievously]` What could possibly go wrong?
More people started dancing. [C09]
So the city panicked. The stages came down. Music and public dancing were banned. At weddings, strings only. No DRUMS. [C10]
The worst dancers were taken in wagons to a saint's shrine… given red shoes… and blessed. [C11]
`[whispers]` It finally stopped. Nobody can agree on why it started. [C13]

*~125 words. Visual comedy: the band playing harder as more people join; the "NO DRUMS" proclamation nailed
to a door, one lonely violinist at a wedding.*
