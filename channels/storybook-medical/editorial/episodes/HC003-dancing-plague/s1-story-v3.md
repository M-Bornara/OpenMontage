## What changed from v2 (Oct 18, before Rule 0, the story doctor and the fact gate)
- **v2 had no twist.** It ended "Strasbourg wasn't so lucky": a mood, not a reversal. v3 brings forward the strongest true turn of the story: the doctors' cure was **more dancing**; the city built stages and paid musicians; one chronicle counts 400 within a month (C07, C08, C19). **Cost:** S2 ("The City Hired Musicians") loses its reveal; S2 is not on the slate, and the long (January) tells it in full. If you'd rather keep it for S2, the fallback ending is in "Option B" below.
- **Four lines fixed by the sources:** "by the end of the summer… four hundred" → "within a month" (C19: the Imlin chronicle); "There, she finally stopped" is out (the essay says both "might be cured" and "had been cured": C18); the collapse and the swollen feet now rest on C16; the crowd on C17.
- **No red shoes in S1:** the red-shoe ritual is told for the dancers sent later, not for Troffea (C20). The motif goes to S2 / the long.

## Spine
- **Who:** Frau Troffea, a woman of Strasbourg (C02; we know almost nothing else about her: no invented thoughts).
- **Wants / against:** to stop? Her husband begs her to; her body won't (C03, C16).
- **Stakes:** her body (collapse, swollen feet: C16), then the whole city (more than thirty, then 400: C04, C19).
- **The choice:** the frightened authorities send her away in a wagon to the shrine of the saint believed to have cursed her (C18, C20).
- **The turn:** the dancing doesn't leave with her (C01, C04).
- **The payoff:** the doctors' cure was more dancing; the city built stages and hired musicians, and one chronicle counts 400 within a month (C07, C08, C19). Nobody knows why it started (C13).
