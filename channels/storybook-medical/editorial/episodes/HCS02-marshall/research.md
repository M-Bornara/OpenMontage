## Script v1 — He Drank Bacteria to Prove a Doctor Wrong
*Working title from the plan. Better: "The Doctor Who Drank Bacteria".*

`[excited]` In 1984, a doctor drank a glass of bacteria. On purpose.
Barry Marshall was a young physician in Perth, Australia. He and pathologist Robin Warren had found strange spiral bacteria living in inflamed stomachs.
Back then, most doctors blamed ulcers on stress and lifestyle.
Marshall's idea? Bacteria. Almost nobody believed him.
`[curious]` He couldn't infect animals to prove it. So he used the only test subject left: himself.
He didn't ask the ethics committee. He didn't tell his wife the details. Easier, he wrote, "to get forgiveness than permission."
Within about a week, he was vomiting. His breath was awful. A biopsy showed the bacteria… and an inflamed stomach.
`[mischievously]` His wife suggested it was time to treat himself.
Twenty-one years later, Marshall and Warren won the Nobel Prize… and ulcers became curable.

*~125 words. "A glass" is narrative shorthand for "a culture", which is acceptable; P2 describes drinking a
culture. Visuals: Perth hospital, 1980s; a microscope with spiral shapes (stylised); a lab flask; a
comic-timing cut to the wife's raised eyebrow; Stockholm.*
