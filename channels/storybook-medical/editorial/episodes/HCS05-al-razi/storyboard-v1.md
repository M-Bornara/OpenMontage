# HCS05 — The Doctor Who Refused to Treat Half His Patients · storyboard v1

Rules: `plan/production-v3.md` (decoupage L28, Matt cameo L29, pre-viz L36/L37), `plan/culture-themes.md`, the Shorts
safe zone (text only in y 288–1248). Facts: `research.md`. Format: 9:16 Short, 1080×1920, Liam. No end sting (loop).
Slot: **Mon Dec 7**.
**Care:** bloodletting shown as a brass bowl and a lancet on cloth, and bandaged forearms; never the cut, never blood
running. Meningitis shown as fever: damp cloths, a hand at a stiff neck, worried family.
**Period:** Rey around 900 AD (Samanid / early Buyid): mud-brick and baked-brick walls, plain brick domes, carved
stucco, wooden lattice; **no blue-tiled domes** (those are centuries later). No authentic portrait of al-Razi
exists: a conventional look, not a likeness.
**Theme:** `persia` (approved) · captions + stamps in **Lalezar** · music: santur, setar, ney, daf + tombak; dastgah
Shur; curious, careful, a little mischievous at "He was wrong"; no Western orchestra.

## Script v1 (approved 2026-11-06, unchanged: ≈ 102 words, ≈ 40 s)

```
[curious] Eleven hundred years ago, a doctor refused to treat some of his patients. On purpose.
Al-Razi was a Persian physician from Rey, near today's Tehran.
For the first signs of meningitis, the treatment was bloodletting.
Al-Razi wasn't sure it worked.
So he bled one group of patients… and deliberately left another group alone.
Then he watched what happened to both.
The bled group did better, he wrote, in his giant medical encyclopedia. So he kept the treatment.
[mischievously] He was wrong. Bloodletting doesn't help meningitis.
[curious] Some call it the first clinical trial. Not quite. But comparing two groups is how we test every medicine today.
```
