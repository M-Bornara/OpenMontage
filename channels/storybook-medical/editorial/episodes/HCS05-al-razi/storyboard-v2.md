# HCS05 — The Doctor Who Refused to Treat Half His Patients · storyboard v2 (story v2 approved 2026-11-15)

Rules: `plan/production-v3.md` (decoupage L28, Matt cameo L29, pre-viz L36/L37), `plan/culture-themes.md`, the Shorts
safe zone (text only in y 288–1248). Facts: `research.md`. Format: 9:16 Short, 1080×1920, Liam. No end sting (loop).
Slot: **Mon Dec 7**.
**Care:** bloodletting shown as a brass bowl and a lancet on cloth, and bandaged forearms; never the cut, never blood
running. Meningitis shown as fever: damp cloths, a hand at a stiff neck, worried family.
**Period:** Rey around 900 AD (Samanid / early Buyid): mud-brick and baked-brick walls, plain brick domes, carved
stucco (Samanid tomb, Nain mosque), wooden lattice; **no blue-tiled domes** (those are centuries later).
**Writing (user note 2026-11-14):** paper, not wood. Baghdad made paper from the late 700s; scholars sat on a carpet
or cushion and wrote on a sheet held on the knee or a low board with a reed pen (qalam) and an inkwell. No desks,
no chairs, no wooden "books". Script: the angular cursive of the 900s (see the c. 950–1000 folio in references.md).
**The Hawi:** compiled after his death by his students from his notebooks (NLM; Encyclopedia.com), so the image is
his stacks of paper notebooks, not one bound book.
**His look:** no portrait from his lifetime survives. We follow the portrait Iranians know (1956 painting for the
5th Iranian Medical Congress, Wellcome S0001954, CC BY 4.0): white turban, full black beard, strong brows, sand
robe with a gold-trimmed collar over a blue tunic.
**Theme:** `persia` (approved) · captions + stamps in **Lalezar** · music: santur, setar, ney, daf + tombak; dastgah
Shur; curious, careful, a little mischievous at "He was wrong"; no Western orchestra.

## Script v2 (approved 2026-11-15: "new scripts looks much better go for it")

```
[curious] Eleven hundred years ago, a doctor refused to treat some of his patients. On purpose.
[serious] Persia. Fever. Stiff necks. The first signs of meningitis.
The standard cure: open a vein, and let the blood out.
[curious] But al-Razi, a physician from Rey, had a doubt. [pause] What if the cure does nothing?
So he split his patients in two. One group, he bled. The other… he left alone.
[whispers] Then he waited… [pause] and watched both.
[curious] The bled group did better, he wrote. So he kept the cure.
[pause] [mischievously] He was wrong. Bloodletting doesn't help meningitis.
[curious] His answer was wrong. But his method, two groups side by side, is how we test every medicine today.
```
