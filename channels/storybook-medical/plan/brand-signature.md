# Oddly Matt — brand signature system (proposal, 2026-10-18)

The idea: viewers should recognise an Oddly Matt film in five seconds, with **no logo inside the story**. The
signatures live *in* the world, the way Hitchcock appeared in his own films.

## The core symbol: Matt's spectacles
- Round wire-rimmed spectacles, a design that exists in every era we cover (from the 1500s to today).
- **Our unique detail:** **amber-tinted lenses**, with a small **"?" glint** in the left lens.
  - Nobody else's glasses look like this, so it's ownable.
  - It's simple enough to work as a 32-pixel profile picture (two circles + a "?").
- One symbol, used three ways:

| Use | How |
|---|---|
| **Hidden object** | In every film the spectacles are hidden in one scene, **as a small visual joke** (see below). This is the "spot it" game in the comments. |
| **Logo** | The spectacles icon = profile picture. "Oddly Matt" wordmark + spectacles = banner, end screen, thumbnails (small corner mark). |
| **Sting** | A 1-second intro or outro: the spectacles land on a book, and the "?" glints. Used at the start of long videos and on the end screen, never inside Shorts' first seconds. |

## The cameo: Matt, the time-travelling onlooker
- A **recurring background character**: the narrator's avatar, quietly present at every story like a
  witness. Examples:
  - a voyageur at the trading post;
  - a passenger on Gage's coach;
  - a face in the Strasbourg crowd;
  - a visitor in a 1929 hospital corridor;
  - a hiker on the ridge in Norway.
- **Look (decided 2026-10-18): "The Quiet Witness".** Tall and slim, long charcoal coat, wide-brimmed felt hat,
  calm small smile, writing in his notebook. His face is shown (eyes visible through the amber lenses): the owner
  tried a version with the eyes hidden in hat shadow and preferred the open face. He is a painted character, not
  a likeness of the owner. Character sheet: `projects/oddly-matt-mascot/assets/images/refs/matt-a-witness.png`
  (Nano Banana Pro, chained from the kit style anchor); every cameo is chained from this sheet.
- **Wardrobe:** dressed for each era (so he never breaks history), but with **three constant signatures**:
  1. the amber spectacles;
  2. a **teal scarf** (a scarf exists in every era; teal is our house colour);
  3. a small **amber notebook** he's always writing in (the storyteller taking notes).
- **Rules:**
  - **Owner decision 2026-10-19: use Matt boldly.** He's the channel's recurring character, the viewer's stand-in
    who keeps turning up in history.
    - **Long videos: 4–6 appearances**, roughly one per chapter, each a small **comic beat**: always in the era's
      clothes, always with something to do.
      - The canoe brigade: he's the one writing instead of paddling.
      - The fur store: he gets a mouthful of the crowd's dust.
      - Washington: he salutes too late.
      - The churchyard: he takes off his hat.
    - **Escalation:** the first appearance is barely noticeable; later ones are easier to catch. Viewers who have
      started looking for him get rewarded.
    - **Shorts: one appearance** in every Short where a background exists. He's the "did you see him?" moment.
    - Each appearance is planned in the shot plan (a "Matt beat" column) with the gag written down, like a line
      of dialogue.
  - He never changes the story, never speaks, is never where a fact is being shown, and isn't mentioned in
    narration. He **may react**: wince, take notes, shrug at the camera once per film.
  - He's an artistic signature, not a historical person. The description says so once in the channel's
    About page ("Spot Matt in every story").
- **Built once:** a character sheet (front, ¾, profile + 3 era outfits), ≈ $0.30 one-time.

## Where the glasses go: a joke, not a shelf (owner note 2026-10-19)
HC001 put them in odd but dull places (on a shelf, a fence rail). Odd is good, because it makes people ask
questions. **Funny is better.** From HC002 on, every placement must pass three tests:
1. **It interacts with the scene:** the glasses are *doing* something.
   - Perched on a sleeping hen.
   - Hooked on the beef cube on the string.
   - Wedged on the face of a portrait on the wall.
   - Floating in a soup pot beside the ladle.
   - Worn by a dog watching the experiment.
   - Hanging from a church bell rope.
2. **It's physically plausible and period-safe:** nothing magical, nothing that makes a claim.
3. **It's findable on a second look:** in the background or midground of a static or slow shot that is held at
   least 3 s. Never during the key fact.

Write the gag in the shot plan (e.g. "glasses on the hen's head, she's unimpressed"). Generate it as part of the
master, not as a separate image.

## What we deliberately don't do
- **No "Oddly Matt" text painted into historical scenes** (signs, papers, posters). It breaks immersion and
  history, and feels like an ad.
- **No more than two signature *types* per film** (the spectacles + Matt). Matt may appear several times. Adding a signature bucket, hat
  and so on as well would turn the scenes into a mascot hunt and pull attention from the story.
  - If a third is ever wanted, the cameo's notebook already plays that role.
- **No burned-in watermark on Shorts** (it hurts reposting on other platforms). Long videos can use
  YouTube's own branding watermark (the spectacles icon, bottom-right).
