# Storybook Medical — launch plan (pre-production)

Status: **planning only — no AI generation until each item's script, shot list and publish texts are approved.**
Date: 2026-10-17. Pilot Shorts A1 and A2 are finished and serve as the quality bar.

## 1. Format and promise

True, verified stories about the human body and medical history, told in 38–48 s illustrated Shorts
(hand-painted 2.5D storybook look, excited storyteller voice, twist ending that loops) and in 7–11 min
long videos that collect a series into one story. General audience 10+, not "made for kids".
Every on-screen fact needs two sources; disputed facts are presented as disputed.

## 2. Content architecture: Shorts first, long videos assembled

Each long video is fed by a series of 3–7 Shorts. Shorts are released first and each one links to its
long video through YouTube's **Related video** field once the long video is live. Because every
keyframe is now produced in **9:16 and 16:9**, the long video reuses the Shorts' art.

| Long video | Format | Fed by Shorts | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** The Man With a Window Into His Stomach — The Full Story | Full-story compilation | A1–A4 | 7–8 min |

| **L3** 7 Doctors Who Experimented on Themselves | List | C1–C7 | 9–11 min |

| **L5** 5 People Whose Bodies Refused to Die | List | E1–E5 | 8–10 min |

Plus **F1–F3**: evergreen standalone body-science Shorts (search traffic, filler days, future list video).

## 4. Long videos

All long videos use a new 16:9 template (**StorybookLong**, built once before L1): 20–30 s cold open
that plays the most shocking moment, chapter title cards, slower pacing (new shot every 3–6 s),
smaller captions + SRT, open loops before each chapter, and a 15–20 s **end screen** with a narrated
"watch next" and reserved space for YouTube's end-screen video + subscribe elements (no loop).
Deliverables add: 3 thumbnail variants (1280×720) for YouTube's Test & Compare, chapter timestamps,
end-screen plan, pinned comment.

**L1 — The Man With a Window Into His Stomach: The Full Story (7–8 min).**
Cold open: the beef on a string. Ch1 The Accident (A1) · Ch2 The Hole That Never Closed · Ch3 The
Experiments (A3) · Ch4 The Servant and the Contract (A2) · Ch5 Running Away · Ch6 Twenty Years of
Letters · Ch7 The Last Surprise (A4) · Outro: what his stomach taught us (digestion is chemical) →
end screen to L3. New: ~12 bridge scenes, extended narration. Thumbnail: Alexis lifting his shirt +
"A WINDOW INTO HIS STOMACH". **≈ $4.**

**L2 — The Dancing Plague of 1518 (8–10 min).**
Cold open: a woman dancing alone in the street. Ch1 Strasbourg, a city in famine · Ch2 Frau Troffea
(B1) · Ch3 Dozens, then hundreds · Ch4 The Cure That Made It Worse (B2) · Ch5 The Shrine of St Vitus
· Ch6 What Really Happened (B3: ergot vs mass psychogenic illness, disputed deaths) · Ch7 It Happened
Again (other dancing manias) → end screen to L4. **≈ $5.**

**L3 — 7 Doctors Who Experimented on Themselves (9–11 min).**
Countdown 7→1 from least to most extreme, each chapter = one C Short widened to 16:9 plus a 30–40 s
"what happened next" bridge. Ends on Forssmann's Nobel. **≈ $5.**

**L4 — Phineas Gage: The Truth About the Man With a Hole in His Head (8–10 min).**
Myth-busting structure: "what your textbook says" vs "what actually happened". Ch1 The Explosion (D1)

**L5 — 5 People Whose Bodies Refused to Die (8–10 min).**
Countdown of E1–E5, each with "the science of why they survived" (cold slows metabolism, etc.);
Vulović's disputed fall presented as an open question. **≈ $5.**

## 6. Pre-production workflow (nothing generated until approved)

For each series, in batches:
1. **Research brief** — facts with two sources, disputed points marked (free).
2. **Script** — Eleven-v3 text with tags and punch words, 105–120 words for Shorts; long-form
   narration ~1,100–1,500 words per video (free).
3. **Shot list** — full `episode.json` (references, keyframes, clips, shots, overlays, sfx) timed
   from the approved narration (narration costs ~$0.07 and is the first paid step).
4. **Publish texts** — titles, descriptions, captions, pinned comments, cover concept (free).
5. ✋ You approve the batch → generation runs series by series so references are shared.

Batch order: **Batch 1** A3, A4, L1 outline · **Batch 2** B1–B3, L2 · **Batch 3** C1–C7, L3 ·
**Batch 4** D1–D3, L4 · **Batch 5** E1–E5, F1–F3, L5.
