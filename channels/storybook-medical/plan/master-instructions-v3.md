## 1. Mission
Evidence-driven storytelling brand about extraordinary true stories: medicine, the human body, survival,
medical history, unusual historical cases, scientific discoveries, disputed historical claims, myths that
became accepted fact, extraordinary documented human experiences.
Historical storytelling + scientific accuracy + visual storytelling + curiosity + human editorial judgment.
Not mass-produced AI videos: a recognizable media property whose videos happen to be produced efficiently
with AI. AI does mechanical production; editorial judgment, factual responsibility, positioning, QC and
publication stay human-controlled.

## 2. Core principle
Research → Story hypothesis → Long-form case → Shorts → Audience evidence → Improve → Repeat → Monetize → Scale.
Not: generate many AI videos → upload everything → hope. Priorities: topic selection, story quality,
packaging, retention, factual credibility, recognizable identity, production economics, repeatability.

Create a naming shortlist that supports medicine, history, survival, science, strange documented events,

## 5–6. Promise and personality
Promise: "True stories of medicine, history and human survival — with the myth separated from the record."
Short banner: "True stories of medicine, history and survival."
Feels: intelligent, curious, cinematic, slightly mysterious, historically grounded, visually beautiful,

## 7–9. Visual identity
Keep the hand-painted 2.5D illustrated storybook aesthetic, but a consistent visual *grammar*, not a rigid
template. Consistent: illustration quality, typography, caption system, colour philosophy, transitions,
lighting philosophy, character rendering, reconstruction style. Varies: environments, composition, camera
movement, palette, pacing, framing, chapter presentation, emotional tone.
No medical clichés (caduceus, red cross, hospital logos, heartbeat lines, cartoon doctors, pills, syringes).
Prefer casebook, archive, open book, historical file, fingerprint, human silhouette, map, timeline,
document, magnifying glass, subtle anatomical motif.
Never "healthier lives"; never imply watching improves health. Historical/educational storytelling, not
medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

## 10–11. Audience and geography
English-speaking teens and adults who like history, science, medicine, mysteries, survival, documentaries,
unusual people, strange events, myth-busting. Do not target children; **Not made for kids**. Avoid childish
narration, toy-like graphics, preschool animation, child-focused metadata, exaggerated cartoon SFX.
Launch in English for US, Canada, UK, Australia and other English audiences (not Canada-only). Stories
increasingly global over time; no quotas during the probe — story quality first.

## 12–14. Narration
Default: a high-quality **licensed AI narrator**. The owner's natural voice is not required; YouTube does
not require human narration for monetization. Use the best-performing licensed AI narrator unless testing
shows another voice performs better. Sound: intelligent, curious, energetic without shouting,
conversational, slightly dramatic, warm, credible, documentary-like — "someone telling you an unbelievable
true story they cannot wait to explain". Avoid robotic, generic TikTok, overly theatrical, movie-trailer,
childish, fake-news, excessively excited.
One primary narrator for the whole experiment. Maintain a pronunciation dictionary (historical names,
medical terms, places, foreign names, scientific terms). Listen to every narration before publication.

## 15. AI disclosure
AI narration does not make a channel inauthentic; what matters is original, meaningfully produced content.
Use YouTube's disclosure when realistic synthetic media could be mistaken for real footage/events. For
illustrated reconstructions add: "Illustrations are AI-assisted artistic reconstructions based on the cited
historical record. Research, scripting, editorial decisions and final production are human-directed."
Do not repeatedly advertise "THIS VIDEO WAS MADE WITH AI". AI is a production method, not the brand.

## 16–24. Human Editorial Standard (seven stages, every video)
1. **Editorial thesis** — Why is this story worth telling? What does our version add over a generic summary
   (myth correction, disputed evidence, overlooked consequence, connected evidence, the science, what
   retellings got wrong)? No answer → reject the topic.
2. **Claim review** — classify every important claim.
   - Tier A central/high-risk (cause of death, experiment count, survival mechanism, disputed claim, medical
     mechanism, central hook): primary evidence + authoritative interpretation, or two strong independent
     scholarly sources when primary evidence is inaccessible.
   - Tier B material context (date, place, occupation, documented event): usually one authoritative source.
   - Tier C narrative connective material: no artificial double sourcing.
   - Tier D medical/actionable (diagnosis, treatment, prevention, safety advice): current authoritative
     medical evidence + review, otherwise remove.
   Source hierarchy: original records/primary documents, peer-reviewed research, scholarly books,
   government, universities, museums/archives, professional bodies, reputable journalism, encyclopedias.
   Blogs, Reddit, TikTok, random YouTube, Wikipedia = leads only.
   Wording: Established "Records show…"; contemporary attribution "Beaumont reported…"; later claim "Later
   accounts claimed…"; disputed "Later accounts claim this happened, but contemporary evidence does not
   establish it."; unknown "Historians still do not know…"; leading explanation "One leading explanation
   is…". Never turn "possible" into "proven".
3. **Full script edit** — AI may draft; the whole script is reviewed for accuracy, logic, originality,
   repetition, pacing, emotional manipulation, unsupported certainty, medical implications, historical
   context. It must feel intentionally authored.
4. **Original contribution** — every long video has at least one: myth correction, evidence disagreement,
   historical context, scientific explanation, ethical context.
5. **Visual review** — reject gore, exposed organs, excessive blood, anatomical impossibilities, historical
   inaccuracies, modern objects, wrong clothing/architecture, distorted hands/faces, text artefacts,
   photoreal fake archival footage (unless intentionally disclosed). Illustrated and cinematic, not fake archive.
6. **Audio review** — pronunciation, pacing, emphasis, pauses, names, dates, terms, AI artefacts.
   Regenerate individual sentences rather than accept poor narration.
7. **Final editorial review** — watch the whole video; confirm claims, captions, sources, music, rights,
   narration, visuals, title, thumbnail, description, chapters, disclosure, audience setting, Related Video,
   playlist, end screen. **No video publishes automatically.**

## 32–36. Formats
Shorts 35–55 s (not forced to 45). 0–1.5 s immediate contradiction (no "Did you know", no "Today we're…");
1.5–7 s person, place, stakes; 7–30 s escalate; 30–45 s reveal (reversal, discovery, myth correction,
consequence); ending = payoff. **Loop only when natural**; no awkward sentences to force a loop.
Visual change every ~1.5–3 s, always supporting the story (moves, close-ups, environments, objects, maps,
documents, diagrams, dates, subtle text); no meaningless constant motion.
Long-form 8–12 min, never stretched. 0–15 s show the mystery; 15–30 s central question; by 30 s viewers know
who, what, why keep watching. Every 45–90 s something meaningful (question, evidence, contradiction,
location, consequence, document, science, myth correction). No fake cliffhangers ("what happened next will
shock you"); use real ones ("Beaumont's notes reveal something stranger…").
Shorts are not chapters copied from the long video; both come from one research package; ≥ ~60% of
long-form narration is material not in the Shorts.

## 81–90. Agent behaviour and writing rules
- Challenge bad ideas. Before production give a verdict: APPROVE / RESEARCH MORE / REWRITE / HOLD / REJECT
  with a short reason.
- Never invent facts: missing evidence → `STATUS: UNVERIFIED`; disagreement → `STATUS: DISPUTED`.
- Distinguish fact from storytelling: no invented thoughts, dialogue, emotions, motivations or
  conversations unless clearly presented as reconstruction and supported.
- Script framework: why care · what happened · why strange · what people believed · what the evidence shows ·
  what happened afterward · why it matters.
- Write question → evidence → complication → new question → evidence → reversal → consequence, never
  fact → fact → fact.
- Hook = person + contradiction + consequence. Never withhold basic information artificially.
- People first: never reduce them to freaks, experiments or bodies; acknowledge relationships and power.
- Long-term direction: extraordinary documented human stories at the intersection of history, science,
  medicine and survival.
- Optimise for "the best story we can responsibly tell at the lowest sensible production cost" — not the
  cheapest video, not the most uploads. AI is the production engine; editorial judgment is the product.
