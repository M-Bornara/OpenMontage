### HCS10 Pettenkofer — "He Drank Cholera to Prove a Point"
- Story spine: an old, famous professor (Max von Pettenkofer, Munich) is sure germs alone don't cause cholera
  (his soil / groundwater theory vs Robert Koch). In 1892 he drinks a cholera culture in front of witnesses.
- To verify: date (7 Oct 1892?), his age (~74?), where the culture came from (Koch's lab / Hamburg outbreak?),
  that he took bicarbonate first to neutralise stomach acid, his symptoms (mild diarrhoea?), whether an assistant
  (Rudolf Emmerich?) repeated it, what each side concluded, and how history judges it today.
- Stakes and twist: he survived and claimed victory; the germ theory still won (why he survived = DISPUTED /
  explanations differ: keep dispute wording).
- Keep OUT: the manner of his death (1901). Not part of this story.
- HERO shot idea: the glass rising to his lips, the liquid swirling (sprite + displacement), the witnesses
  flinching.
- Pair: Marshall (drank bacteria, 1984) → pinned-comment hook "Who was braver?".

### HCS11 Jean Hilliard — "Frozen Solid, She Woke Up"
- Story spine: a 19-year-old drives home on a freezing night in rural Minnesota, the car goes into a ditch, she
  walks for help, collapses metres from a friend's door, is found hours later frozen stiff, and recovers.
- To verify: date (20 Dec 1980?), place (near Lengby / Fosston, Minnesota?), temperature that night, distance
  walked, the friend's name (Wally Nelson?), hours outside (~6?), body temperature on arrival, the doctors'
  descriptions ("frozen solid", skin too hard for a needle?), length of stay, outcome (no amputations?).
  Sources: contemporary newspaper reports, later interviews with her and the doctors; no forum retellings.
- Privacy: she is a private person; use only what she and the press made public.
- HERO shot idea: frost creeping over the frame (mask + particles), her hand reaching for the door, breath
  stopping; later the colour warming back (colour shift trick).
- Pair: Bågenholm (Nov 19) → "Impossible Survival" mini-series feel.

### HCS12 Stubbins Ffirth — "The Doctor Who Drank Yellow Fever Vomit"
- Story spine: a young medical student in Philadelphia sets out to prove yellow fever isn't contagious, and
  exposes himself to patients' black vomit in more and more extreme ways. He never gets sick, so he thinks he is
  right. He was wrong for a reason nobody knew yet: the mosquito (callback to Finlay, Dec 3).
- To verify: years (1802–1804?), his thesis (UPenn 1804?), each exposure (cuts in the arm, eyes, drinking it?),
  whether samples were from late-stage patients, and how historians explain why he never got sick.
- Gross factor: describe, never show. No vomit on screen; show the bowl, his face, the cut, the thesis page.
- HERO shot idea: his hand hesitating over the cup, candle flicker, the cup tipping (puppet bend + wiggle).
- Fallback: Koepcke (see above).
