# Oddly Matt: growth strategy v1 (2026-10-26)

Written after HC001-L was scheduled. It adds evidence to [`master-instructions-v3.md`](master-instructions-v3.md)
(v3.1 still governs) and changes four things: the publish times, a soft bridge line at the end of Case Shorts,
a visible fact rating on every story, and a world slate of documented medical/science stories that starts with
Persia and the Middle East.
Review it at the 28-day mark (Dec 6) and at day 90 (Feb 6, 2027).

## 1. What the evidence says (and how reliable it is)

| Finding | Source | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts and long videos run on separate recommendation systems; YouTube hasn't seen Shorts hurt long-form | YouTube (Todd Beaupré, Creator Insider, Sep 2026, via secondary coverage) | strong |
| Publish time is "not known to impact a video's long-term performance"; many videos get most views a month+ later; the first hour mostly reaches regular viewers | YouTube FAQ + Beaupré | strong |
| New channels: YouTube matches the video's TOPIC to viewers interested in it; if nobody interested responds, impressions stop | Beaupré | strong |
| Subscribers click maybe 10% of uploads, so the subscribe button matters less than people think | Beaupré | strong |
| Related Video link: point at matching content, add a spoken/visual CTA in the Short's last ~5 s, and the long video must pay the promise in its first 5–10 s | YouTube Blog guide | strong (official) |
| Shorts-to-long viewer overlap is low; Related Video click rates are often reported at about 1% | creator-tool blogs | weak (direction is consistent) |
| Shorts ≥ ~40 s that hold their audience do best; Shorts drive subscribers more than revenue | Paddy Galloway, 3.3B-view study (2023) | medium (dated) |
| "More videos don't grow channels; better videos do"; 5+ thumbnail ideas, make 3 | Galloway | medium |
| TV viewing is growing and TV viewers want longer videos | Beaupré | strong; fits history/documentary |

**What this means for us:** the long video is the asset, because it earns the watch hours (YPP: 8,000 h), comes up
in search and suggested for years, and gets watched on TV. A Short is a cheap test of a topic and a source of
subscribers. Don't count on Shorts sending many people to the long video; each must pay off on its own.

## 2. The model: "story universes" plus a weekly standalone Short

- **One long video every two weeks.** Only make a long video when the Story Expansion Map shows character, conflict
  and aftermath. In the strongest week, a second long video is fine, but never padded.
- **Each universe gets 1–3 Case Shorts.** Make only as many as the story has real standalone angles. Three is a
  ceiling, not a quota.
- **One standalone Story Short every week from a Short-only topic.** This tests topics cheaply ($2–4). Promote a topic
  to a long video when its Short beats the channel median by about 2× on "stayed to watch" and shares.
- **Steady state is about 3 Shorts and 0.5 long videos per week.** That's about 1–2 hours of your time, as in
  upload-schedule.md. Ramp up after day 90 only if the 28-day reviews show demand.
- **Own one sub-niche first:** *illustrated true medical stories with a fact rating*. Branch out (survival, strange
  history) only from what wins.

## 3. Changes to the formats

1. **Soft bridge line in Case Shorts (new, test on half of them).** After the payoff, add one line of about 3 s,
   e.g. "His next 58 years were even stranger. The full story is linked." Keep the rule that a Short is never a
   trailer: the story is finished before the bridge. Compare Related-Video clicks with and without it.
2. **The long video pays off its promise in the first 5–10 s.** We already do this (the hook flash-forwards). Keep it.
3. **Fact meter on every story (new).** It's a small on-screen tag that maps to our claim tiers, shown when a doubtful
   claim appears, and it closes every Myth vs Fact segment:
   - **DOCUMENTED:** primary sources (Beaumont's journal, letters, excavation reports).
   - **LIKELY:** a good secondary source, or a near-contemporary account.
   - **DISPUTED:** sources disagree. Say both sides.
   - **LEGEND:** written down long after the event, or a literary or poetic source. Tell it as a legend, and say so.

   This lets us tell older stories, where the documents are thin, without lying. It's also part of the brand: viewers
   who care about accuracy will trust us, and the comments will argue about the ratings (good engagement).
4. **Packaging stays the main lever.** Keep 3 thumbnails per long video, title and thumbnail written before the script,
   and a movie-poster cover for each Short.

## 4. The world slate (start: Persia and the Middle East)

**Scope (user decision, 2026-10-26):** this channel tells **medical and scientific** true stories about real people
and real discoveries, from every culture, and only stories with a **documented core**. A story qualifies when its
central events are DOCUMENTED or LIKELY. Legends appear only as a labelled **Myth vs Fact segment inside a real
story**, never as a whole video. Pure myths and epics (Rostam's birth, the Shahnameh) don't belong here. They are
parked for a possible later playlist or sister channel, which would need its own format, because a story that is
legend from start to finish can't be carried by a fact meter.

| Story | Where / when | Format | Core rating | The legend inside it (Myth vs Fact) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The 5,000-year-old artificial eye | Shahr-e Sukhteh (Burnt City), Sistan, c. 2900–2800 BC | Story Short | DOCUMENTED find (excavation 2006); date, material and her role DISPUTED | "she was a priestess" (popular claim, no source) |
| The doctor who tested bloodletting on two groups | al-Razi, Rey / Baghdad, c. 900 CE | Story Short | DOCUMENTED passage (Kitab al-Hawi) | "the first clinical trial ever" (an overstatement) |
| Avicenna: the boy who wrote medicine's textbook | Bukhara → Hamadan, 980–1037; the Canon used in Europe for ~500 years | **Long candidate** | DOCUMENTED (his autobiography, the Canon) | the lovesick prince and the pulse test (LEGEND, told ~120 years later), as one segment |
| The smallpox trick Europe learned from Istanbul | Lady Mary Wortley Montagu, 1717–1721 | **Long candidate** | DOCUMENTED (her letters, the royal trial) | "she invented vaccination" (no: that was Jenner, later) |
| The discovery that was lost for 300 years | Ibn al-Nafis, Cairo, 13th c. (pulmonary circulation) | Long or Short | DOCUMENTED manuscript | "Europe copied him" (influence DISPUTED) |

**Out of scope here:** Rostam's birth (myth/epic), the al-Razi hospital-site test (told ~300 years later; at most a
Myth vs Fact line in a Razi story), al-Razi going blind (legend only, at most a closing line in a
Razi story), Democedes (Herodotus only; HOLD), and the Jundishapur legends (HOLD).

**Around the world next** (same rule, a documented medical/scientific core): China, India, Africa and Latin America,
one story each in the first 90 days, chosen by the Story Expansion Map. Examples to research: Onesimus and the 1721
Boston inoculation (an enslaved African man's knowledge), the Sushruta Samhita nose reconstruction, Hua Tuo
(mostly legend, so likely out), Carlos Finlay and yellow fever.

Next steps: research packages for the artificial eye and the Razi Short (cheap, fits the current pipeline), plus
Story Expansion Maps for Avicenna and Montagu as possible long videos. Then let the 28-day numbers decide.

## 5. Publish times: plan around Eastern Time

You're in Mountain Time. About half of the US and Canadian audience lives in Eastern or Central Time, so ET is the
right reference. Be honest about the size of this lever, though: YouTube says the publish hour isn't known to change
long-term results, and Shorts are spread over days. What timing can do is make the video available when the first
interested viewers are online, and keep us consistent.

| Slot | ET (plan) | Alberta, winter (ET − 1) | Alberta, summer from Mar 14 (ET − 2) | US Pacific |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long video, Sunday | **11:00** | 10:00 | 9:00 | 8:00 |
| Shorts, Mon / Wed / Fri | **15:00** | 14:00 | 13:00 | 12:00 |
| TikTok / Instagram copies (from cluster 2) | 18:00 | 17:00 | 16:00 | 15:00 |

Alberta stays on UTC-6 all year from Nov 2026, so only the Alberta columns change with the US clock change; the ET
plan never moves.

The long video stays as scheduled: 11:00 ET on Sunday is a strong slot for the whole continent. The Shorts move from
17:00 MT (19:00 ET) to 13:00 MT, so they go live mid-afternoon in ET, in the window most studies point to, and are
already moving when Pacific evenings start. After about 4 weeks, YouTube Studio's "When your viewers are on YouTube"
replaces this table.

## 6. What we measure (unchanged, with sharper triggers)

- **Long video, 7 days:** CTR under ~4% with good retention → change the packaging (swap the thumbnail or title).
  First-30-s retention under ~65% → fix the opening of the next video.
- **Short:** "stayed to watch" under ~60% → the first second didn't land. A high stay rate with few shares → the
  ending didn't land.
- **Day 28 and day 90:** which universe and which fact-rating style wins decides cluster 4 and the world slate order.
