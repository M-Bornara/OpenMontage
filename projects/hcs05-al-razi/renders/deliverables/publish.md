## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** 1,100 Years Ago, a Doctor Refused to Treat Patients on Purpose

**Description:**
```
Rey, near today's Tehran, around 900 AD. For the first signs of meningitis, the standard treatment was bloodletting. The Persian physician Abu Bakr al-Razi (c. 865–925) had a doubt: what if it did nothing? So he bled one group of patients and deliberately left another group untreated, to compare them.

He wrote that the bled group did better, and kept the treatment. He was wrong: bloodletting doesn't help meningitis. The passage survives in his Kitab al-Hawi, which his students compiled from his notebooks after his death, and it is the only passage in it that compares two groups of patients.

Some call it the first clinical trial. That's an overstatement, but his method, two groups side by side, is how every medicine is tested today. This is a history story, not medical advice.

More true medical stories: "The Man With a Window Into His Stomach" (linked above).

SOURCES
```
