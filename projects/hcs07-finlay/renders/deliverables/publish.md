## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** He Was Right About the Mosquito. It Took 19 Years.

**Description:**
```
Havana, 1881. Yellow fever was killing thousands, and nobody knew how it spread. On 14 August 1881 Dr Carlos Finlay told the Havana Academy of Sciences that a mosquito carried it. Almost nobody believed him, and for nineteen years he kept breeding mosquitoes and testing his idea.

In 1900 a US Army commission led by Walter Reed came to Cuba and finally tested it, with mosquitoes from Finlay's own eggs. One of the team, Dr Jesse Lazear, was bitten and died of yellow fever. (How he was bitten is disputed: he said a stray mosquito bit him by accident, but a notebook entry suggests he may have infected himself on purpose.) The commission showed the mosquito was the carrier: Finlay had been right all along.

Reed got most of the fame, but Reed himself gave the credit to Finlay. Finlay was born on 3 December 1833, and Latin America marks 3 December as Medicine Day in his honour. This is a history story, not medical advice.

More true medical stories: "The Man With a Window Into His Stomach" (linked above).
```
