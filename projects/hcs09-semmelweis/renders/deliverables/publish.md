## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** They Hated Him for Saying "Wash Your Hands"

**Description:**
```
1847, Vienna General Hospital. It had two maternity clinics, taking women on alternate days. In the First, run by doctors and medical students, about one mother in ten died of childbed fever; in the Second, run by midwives, far fewer. Women begged not to be sent to the First.

Then Ignaz Semmelweis's friend Jakob Kolletschka was cut by a student's scalpel during an autopsy and died with the same signs as the mothers. The doctors were going from the dissecting room straight to the deliveries. In May 1847 Semmelweis made them wash in chlorinated lime, and by July deaths in the First Clinic fell to about one in a hundred.

Many senior doctors rejected his idea, though some did adopt the washing, and he published his full case only in 1861. His post in Vienna ended in March 1849 and was not renewed. In 1865 he was committed to an asylum, where he died two weeks later of an infected wound (whether it came from a beating there isn't proven). Today, rejecting new evidence because it clashes with what we already believe is called the Semmelweis reflex. This is a history story, not medical advice.

More true medical stories: "The Man With a Window Into His Stomach" (linked above).
```
