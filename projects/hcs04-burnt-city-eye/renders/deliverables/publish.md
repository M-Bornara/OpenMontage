## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** She Was Buried With a Golden Eye

**Description:**
```
Around 2900 BC, at Shahr-e Sukhteh, the "Burnt City" in south-east Iran, a woman in her late twenties was buried with an artificial eye. It is a half-ball about 2.5 cm across, made of tar-like bitumen paste under a thin layer of gold, with an iris and fine lines like rays of light drawn on it. Tiny holes on both sides held it in place with a fine thread. It was found in her left eye socket, and under a microscope the socket showed an imprint: she had worn it for a long time.

Was she a priestess, or a queen? That part is a legend: no source says so. And some archaeologists think it was an eyepatch, not an eye. Either way, 5,000 years ago, someone made her a golden eye.

More true medical stories: "The Man With a Window Into His Stomach" (linked above).
```
